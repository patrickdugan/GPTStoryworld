# Kalam Research Brief: Mu'tazili vs Ash'ari Exam Tensions

This brief is a compact source-grounding card for storyworld generation, not a scholarly authority by itself. Use it to stimulate doctrine-aware exam questions and dramatic dilemmas, then keep validator/human review in the loop.

## Core Contrast

Mu'tazili kalam tends to foreground divine unity, divine justice, rational moral responsibility, and human accountability. A common dramatic axis is that if God is just, human beings must be meaningfully responsible for their voluntary acts; otherwise punishment and reward become morally opaque.

Ash'ari kalam tends to protect divine omnipotence and transcendence by stressing that God is the ultimate creator of events and causal efficacy. Human responsibility is framed through acquisition or appropriation of acts rather than independent creative power.

## Divine Attributes

Mu'tazili arguments often resist making divine attributes real entities distinct from the divine essence, because multiplying eternal realities risks compromising tawhid. Anthropomorphic descriptions are often interpreted figuratively or as attributes/actions in relation to creation.

Ash'ari arguments affirm real divine attributes while refusing crude anthropomorphism. This creates exam dilemmas around whether a student can affirm hearing, seeing, speech, power, and will without either multiplying gods or emptying scripture of meaning.

## Created Qur'an / Divine Speech

Mu'tazili reasoning is associated with the view that the Qur'an as God's speech is created, because treating speech as co-eternal can look like adding another eternal beside God.

Ash'ari reasoning distinguishes temporal recitation/letters/sounds from eternal divine speech or meaning. Dramatically, this creates a courtroom problem: is a vow, testimony, or recitation binding because of created utterance, eternal meaning, public form, or intention?

## Causation And Agency

Mu'tazili-inflected cases can ask whether a thief, soldier, judge, or ruler can be blameworthy if every human act is directly created by God.

Ash'ari-inflected cases can ask whether created secondary causes have independent efficacy, or whether apparent causation is a pattern of divine creation. This gives strong storyworld choices around evidence, medicine, fire, famine, and legal responsibility.

## Exam Question Patterns

- Ask the student to decide whether divine justice requires human origination of voluntary acts.
- Ask whether attributes such as speech, knowledge, power, and will are identical with essence, distinct from essence, or only safe when named without asking how.
- Ask whether a written Qur'anic verse in evidence is created ink, created sound, eternal meaning, or all of these in different senses.
- Ask whether a ruler may punish a commanded soldier if all power belongs to God.
- Ask whether a miracle, habit, or cause should count as legal evidence.
- Ask when figurative interpretation protects tawhid and when it becomes evasive.

## Storyworld Use

The best scenes should not ask for doctrinal trivia only. Each exam question should force a ruling that affects a person: a prisoner, widow, water carrier, physician, scribe, soldier, oath-maker, or accused heretic. The secret route should reward the player for seeing that both schools are partly serving different safety functions: one guards justice from arbitrary power, the other guards God from being reduced to a creaturely mechanism.
